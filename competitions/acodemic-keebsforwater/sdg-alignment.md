# SDG alignment

Primary goal: SDG 10, Reduced Inequalities.

Primary target: 10.2, promote social, economic, and political inclusion for all.

## The barrier this build addresses

People who do not share a language can be left out of ordinary exchanges: finding a place, asking for water, stating a preference, or refusing a request. A conventional translator assumes both languages are already known. This project does not. It gives two people a shared record of what was shown, what each person thought it meant, and which follow-up check would separate those readings.

The first audience is newcomers with local volunteers, people working across a language barrier, and language or field researchers who need the disagreement kept intact.

## How the workflow supports inclusion

- Either person can take part without typing a full sentence. A drawing, a gesture note, or a short reading is enough.
- The second person answers before seeing a suggested meaning, so the first person's language does not become the default.
- The facilitator label only guides the steps. The participant can record ambiguity, and approval of a check does not assign a meaning.
- Consent and personal boundaries are one of the guided situations. The request stops if the other person signals no.
- The impact page counts what this session actually recorded and says that a prototype does not show a reduction in inequality.

## What is not claimed

A completed session is not evidence that inequality has fallen. No adoption numbers, testimonials, or field results are included, because none were collected for this submission. The illustrative sample session SHORE-1001 is labelled as a sample. It is not user research.
